The problem

Custom products still break between the buyer and the machine

Uncontrolled input

A phone photo or a vague idea is not a file a laser, router or printer can run.

Manual translation

A designer redraws it, a shop quotes it, and proofs travel back and forth by email for days.

Preview is not product

What looks right on screen can fail on a specific machine, material and thickness, and someone pays for the remake.

Target customer

People and businesses who need a custom physical product made from a photo, name or idea

First

Gift and home shoppers

Buy personalized night lights, wall decor, toys, constructors and banners for gifts, homes, kids and events. Today they choose between generic print-on-demand or slow email orders from a local shop.

Core repeat

Professionals who order for clients

Photographers, event planners, realtors and gift shops. One consumer buys once a year; a photographer with forty sessions a month orders every week.

Later

Merchants and workshops

Etsy and Shopify sellers of custom cut, engraved or printed goods who use our workflow as software, and regional factories that receive validated jobs.

Geography: Armenia first as production base and test market, then English-speaking online demand in the EU and the US with a partner factory in each region. Digital files sell worldwide from day one.

Partnerships and going global

Asset-light from day one: we write the production package, partners execute it

Today

Partner workshop, Armenia

Not owned by us. Our co-founder runs production there with working access to 14 profiled machines. It has produced all ~370 orders to date.

Today

Local manufacturers

Electronics, bases and finishing are delegated to other Armenian shops per order. Our first live test of writing a package another shop can execute.

Next

One EU and one US partner

Formalize the Armenian workshop as the first certified producer of the hero product, then qualify a partner factory near buyers in each region.

Three lanes: global digital files and merchant software sold everywhere; standardized physical products made regionally; bespoke banners, furniture and metal kept local and quoted.
